\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether register tokens regularize a vision transformer when data is
scarce, and answered it with a fully paired twelve-run ablation in which the
register count was the only variable. The answer is no. No register
configuration improves held-out Top-1 accuracy over the register-free control
($75.19 \pm 0.24\%$), and the best register arm, $K=4$, is statistically
indistinguishable from it on held-out loss.

The study's more transferable result is how nearly it concluded the opposite.
On the validation split, $K=4$ narrows the generalization gap in every seed and
lowers validation loss in every seed at $p = 0.024$---a textbook regularization
signature. It vanishes on the test split, and the training loss, unchanged
across all four arms to within $0.005$ nats, shows why: nothing was traded, so
nothing was regularized. A gap measured on the split that also selects the
checkpoint is not evidence about generalization.

At $K=8$ we find the one consistent held-out effect in the study, and it is a
cost: test loss degrades in all three seeds ($p = 0.014$) and final-block
attention entropy falls in all three. On a $192$-dimensional backbone, eight
tokens that carry no image content are not free.

We read this as a scope result rather than a refutation. Registers were
introduced as a pretraining-time intervention at foundation scale; we applied
them at fine-tuning time to a small pretrained model on 9{,}000 images, and the
attention statistics indicate the pretrained routing simply did not reorganise
in that budget. The experiment that would separate ``registers do not help
here'' from ``registers cannot be added late'' is a from-scratch replication at
this scale, and our released sweep infrastructure runs it unchanged.
