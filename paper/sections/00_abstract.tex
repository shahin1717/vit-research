\begin{abstract}
Register tokens were introduced to remove high-norm attention artifacts in
large vision transformers trained on hundreds of millions of images. Their
effect in the opposite regime---a small model with too little data to fit
comfortably---has not been characterised. We ask whether registers act as a
structural regularizer when data is scarce. We run a fully paired ablation on a
9{,}000-image CIFAR-100 subset (100 images per class): ViT-Tiny with
$K \in \{0, 1, 4, 8\}$ register tokens, three seeds per arm, twelve runs in
total, every hyperparameter fixed across arms. On the untouched 10{,}000-image
test split no register configuration improves Top-1 accuracy over the
register-free control ($75.19 \pm 0.24\%$); the closest arm, $K=4$, reaches
$75.06 \pm 0.33\%$, and its $+0.11$ pp Top-5 lead over the control is not
resolvable at this sample size ($p = 0.681$). The regularization signal that motivated the study does
appear, but only on the validation split: $K=4$ lowers validation loss in
3 of 3 seeds ($\Delta = -0.034$, $p = 0.024$) and narrows the generalization gap
in 3 of 3 seeds ($\Delta = -0.037$, $p = 0.051$), while its held-out test loss
is statistically indistinguishable from the control ($\Delta = +0.002$,
$p = 0.823$). Training loss is unchanged across all four arms to within
$0.005$ nats, so the narrower gap is produced entirely by the validation term
and not by reduced fitting. At $K=8$ the picture is consistent with capacity
dilution: test loss degrades in 3 of 3 seeds ($\Delta = +0.020$, $p = 0.014$)
and final-block attention entropy falls in 3 of 3 seeds. We conclude that in
this regime registers do not regularize a ViT-Tiny, that an apparent
regularization effect measured on a small validation split can fail to
transfer, and that the benefit reported at foundation scale does not
automatically survive the transfer to small models fine-tuned on scarce data.
\end{abstract}
